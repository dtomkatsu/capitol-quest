# w7-storm

## A · start
The Kona low sat over the chain for three days.

By the time it moved off, Kauaʻi had lost two highway bridges, Maui had six hundred homes with water through them, and the Kāneʻohe side of Oʻahu was a slow brown river with cars in it. Nobody died, which everyone keeps saying out loud, in the tone people use when they are not yet sure they believe it.

The Governor declares an emergency Sunday night. The federal declaration follows on Tuesday, which covers a large share of it eventually, on a reimbursement schedule measured in years. What it does not cover, and what cannot wait for years, is the state's own share: emergency housing, the two bridges, debris removal, the water systems on three islands, and the harbor.

Budget and Finance puts a working number on it Wednesday morning. Six hundred million dollars.

- **Policy Brain:** Six hundred million against a budget that was already eight hundred million short. That is not a line item — it is the whole argument of your session, restated at gunpoint.
- **Procedure:** This will move as an emergency appropriation and it will move FAST, ahead of everything, because it has to. Every hour Finance spends on it is an hour not spent on your notice.
- **Base Building:** Six hundred families in shelters in Wailuku tonight. Whatever this does to the arithmetic, that is what the arithmetic is about.
- **Talk Story:** Watch what happens to the phrase 'we can't afford it' this week. It is about to get very cheap, and it is about to get very expensive.

### 1. Say the obvious thing: this is what revenue is FOR.
You say it Wednesday, in front of a camera, while the water is still in people's houses. Half the building thinks you are the only honest person in it and the other half thinks you just used a disaster as a talking point, and both halves say so.

- effect: +5 support, -3 influence
- then: B

### 2. Say nothing this week. Send the coalition's vans to Wailuku.
Eleven vans, four days, no press release. On Friday a member you have never gotten a meeting with texts your organizer directly to ask where to send her church's donations.

- effect: +3 support, +2 influence
- then: B

## B · cost
The emergency appropriation passes both chambers inside nine days, which is the fastest anything will move all session. Nobody votes against it. Nobody would.

And on Friday the FY2027 sheet is reissued with the state share in it, and everything you have been carrying since January is now being weighed against a number six hundred million dollars worse than the one you started from.

Nothing in your package changed. The floor under it did.

- **Policy Brain:** Nine days, unanimous, no hearing worth the name. Money moves at exactly the speed the building agrees it has to — remember that the next time somebody tells you the process is why your bill is slow.
- **Procedure:** Your bills are all still alive and all still exactly where they were. What moved is the standard they will be judged against in conference.
- **Educator:** This is the lesson nobody teaches in a civics class: a budget is not a document, it is a running argument, and the weather gets a vote.

### 1. Take the number.
Six hundred million dollars, on your scoreboard, in a column you did not put it in. It is going to sit there for the rest of the session.

- effect: -$600M revenue
- then: end
