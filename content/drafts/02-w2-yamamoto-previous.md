## A · start
Her door is already open when you get there, which after two weeks of scheduled fifteen-minute slots feels almost suspicious. "Jon doesn't do the meet-and-greet circuit," she says, not looking up from a stack of referral folders three feet high. "Somebody on Finance should. Sit — tell me what you actually need, not the one-pager version."

- **Talk Story:** Co-chairs carry the workload the chair doesn't have time for. She reads more bills than he does; she just doesn't get to keep any of them.
- **Procedure:** She can't schedule a hearing — that pen is his, not hers. What she CAN do is tell you things before he decides anything.

### 1. Give her the real number, not the press version.
She writes it down on the folder itself, not a legal pad — in this building, that is the difference between a note and a record.

## B · office
**Rep. Jennifer Yamamoto:** She finally looks up. "Before this, I was a committee analyst — six years, Ways and Means, Senate side. I wrote the fiscal notes Souza's office actually reads." She taps the folder stack. "Jon schedules. I read. Between the two of us we see everything that comes through this room, and half of what happens in the other one." A pause. "You'll want something from over there eventually. Everybody does. I still owe a few people phone calls I never made."

- **Policy Brain:** A former WAM analyst reading your fiscal note is not the same as a stranger reading it. She will know exactly which number is soft before you do.
- **Procedure:** "Owes some people phone calls" is an offer with the ask left off. Don't make her spell out the rest in front of anyone.
- **Talk Story:** She hands you her own history inside of ten minutes. That is either instinct or a test, and it doesn't much matter which — you answer it the same way.

### 1. "What should the fiscal note actually say?"
She pulls a pen and marks up your draft live, the way she must have marked up hundreds for a chair who never once said thank you. "This clears their staff review. Whether it clears Souza is a different problem — that one isn't mine to solve."

- effect: +3 influence
- then: end
