# ev-forecast

## A · start
Rumor from the tax department: the Council on Revenues may shave the forecast at its next meeting. If it's true, every bill with a price tag gets harder. The coalition wants to know the campaign's posture before the papers ask.

- **Procedure:** A rumor isn't a motion. Nothing on the floor changes until the Council actually meets and votes the number.
- **Talk Story:** The building is already reading your posture. Silence is a message too — make sure it's the one you mean.
- **Policy Brain:** 'May shave the forecast' is not a number. Somewhere there is a worksheet with a number on it. Ask for the worksheet.

### 1. Go public first: "This is exactly why we fix revenue."
You beat the rumor to the microphone. The clip travels. So does your name, attached to the word 'taxes.'

- effect: +4 support, -2 influence
- then: end

### 2. Brief the steering committee quietly. Keep powder dry.
Doors close, voices lower. The building notices a campaign that can keep its own counsel.

- effect: +3 influence
- then: end

### 3. [Policy Brain 10 white] Say nothing yet. Ask the department for the actual worksheet.
- mods: PROCEDURE/4
Pass:
Three pages of tables. The shave is 0.4 percent, not the two the rumor grew on the way over. You walk into the steering meeting with the real number and watch the room recalibrate around you.

- effect: +2 support, +3 influence, flag read-the-worksheet
Fail:
You quote a line from the wrong column at the wrong meeting. It is corrected, gently, by someone junior to you.

- effect: -3 influence
