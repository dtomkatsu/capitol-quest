# w8-crossover

## A · start
Crossover.

Everything the House is going to send the Senate this year has to pass third reading by Thursday, and everything it does not send is dead until January. The 48-hour rule means the bills had to be printed and on the desks by Tuesday, which means the real deadline was two days before the deadline, which is a sentence that has ended more campaigns than any vote ever taken.

The floor calendar runs to ninety-odd measures. They will move most of them in a day and a half, with the clerk reading numbers and members voting on titles they last read in committee, if then.

- **Procedure:** The 48-hour decking rule is not a formality — it is why the fight is over before the vote. By the time anyone is voting, the argument is two days cold.
- **Talk Story:** A member who votes yes on crossover is doing you a small favour and telling the other chamber it is now their problem. Do not mistake it for a commitment.
- **Policy Brain:** Whatever crosses, crosses at whatever size it came out of Finance. Nobody is going to make your bill bigger in the Senate. This is the ceiling.

### 1. Whip the floor. Every office, both sides of the aisle, two days.
Forty-some doors. You get eleven real conversations, four of which are with members who were always yes and one of which is with somebody who tells you something true about a colleague and asks you never to say where you heard it.

- effect: +2 influence, +1 support
- then: B

### 2. Fill the gallery. Let them vote with the room watching.
Two hundred people in a gallery built for ninety, in matching shirts, silent because the sergeant-at-arms asked them to be, which is somehow louder. It is on the news at six.

- effect: +5 support, -2 influence
- then: B

### 3. [Procedure 12 white] Stop working the House. Start working the Senate NOW, before it crosses.
- mods: TALK STORY/3, flag souza-intro +2 "Kawaguchi's phone call"
- then: B
Pass:
It is the counterintuitive move and it is the right one: the bill's fate in the Senate is being decided this week by people who have not been asked about it yet. By Thursday two WAM offices have already read it — not because it crossed, but because you got there before it did.

- effect: +6 senate, +3 influence, unlock talk sen-x=talk-sen
Fail:
You spend crossover week in the wrong building. The Senate offices are polite and unhurried and entirely uninterested in a measure that has not reached them, and behind you the House votes without you in the hallway.

- effect: -3 influence

## B · after
Thursday afternoon the clerk finishes reading, the gavel comes down, and every measure that made it walks across the rotunda to a chamber where nobody has ever heard of it, nobody sponsored it, and nobody owes it anything at all.

Half the session is behind you. The half in front of you starts from zero.

- **Procedure:** New chamber, new committee, new chair, new deadlines. The record you built in the House is worth exactly what the Senate decides it is worth, which is usually nothing.
- **Base Building:** Your people just won something. Tell them. A campaign that only reports losses trains everyone to stop reading.
