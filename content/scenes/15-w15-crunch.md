# w15-crunch

## A · start
Conference. Room 309, and you are not in it.

Six people — three from each chamber, named Monday — sitting at a table with the House draft on one side and the Senate draft on the other, and a deadline Friday. There is no public testimony. There is no hearing. There is a clock and a stack, and at some point after ten at night somebody says a number and somebody else says a smaller number.

Rep. Silva has been in that hallway since Tuesday morning. She is not in the room either. She does not have to be; she has been in this building for thirty years.

- **Procedure:** Conference cannot add anything. It can only cut, merge, or let something die by running out the clock — and running out the clock is what it does most.
- **Policy Brain:** Everything gets decided against one figure now, and that figure has six hundred million dollars of storm in it. Your number is not competing with zero. It is competing with a bridge on Kauaʻi.
- **Talk Story:** The conferees are named. That is the entire public record you get. Every other thing you learn this week, you learn from somebody who should not have told you.
- **Base Building:** This is the week the campaign either has durability or does not, and there is nothing left to do about which. Whatever you built by now is what walks into that room with your bill.

### 1. Hold the number. No deal that cuts it, and say so out loud.
You tell all six conferees, in writing, that the coalition will not support a diluted version. It is a real position honestly held and it is also a gun pointed at your own foot, and every person you hand it to understands both halves.

- effect: +5 support, -4 influence, flag held-the-number
- then: B

### 2. Take the deal. Something at sixty percent beats nothing at a hundred.
You let it be known, quietly, that you can live with a smaller number. It is in front of the conferees within the hour, which tells you exactly how closely somebody was listening for it.

- effect: +4 influence, -3 support, flag took-the-deal
- then: B

### 3. [Talk Story 13 red] Find Rep. Silva in the hallway and ask her what she is actually protecting.
- mods: influence/25, POLICY BRAIN/4
- then: B
Pass:
She tells you, because at this hour of this week it costs her nothing and she has always slightly enjoyed you. It is not your bill. It is one clause in it, worth a fraction of the total, and she has been spending a week of a thirty-year reputation on it. You have been defending the whole thing against a woman who only ever wanted one paragraph.

- effect: +5 influence
Fail:
"Long week," she says, and means it, and asks after your family, and means that too, and tells you nothing at all. She is very good at this. It is genuinely a pleasure to watch, right up until you are back in the hallway with what you came with.

- effect: -2 influence
