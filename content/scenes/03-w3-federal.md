# w3-federal

## A · start
The joint briefing runs in a hearing room too small for it, which is how you can tell the department expected fewer people. What Congress did to the safety net in HR1 is no longer a forecast — every piece of it is already law and most of it is already running.

SNAP-Ed is gone: over $2.5 million a year the state used to spend on nutrition classes and farmers'-market programs, zeroed out in October 2025. Work requirements now reach ages 55 to 64 — 16,570 adults in Hawaiʻi who were exempt a year ago — plus 10,300 households with teenagers. On Medicaid, narrower eligibility and twice-yearly recertification have already dropped an estimated 1,200 to 2,400 people from the rolls, not because they stopped qualifying but because they missed a form.

None of it appears as a federal line item anymore. It appears here, in a state budget that is already $800 million short.

- **Policy Brain:** Read the caseload numbers, not just the caseload story. 16,570 and 10,300 are Hawaiʻi's own — DHS ran them, not a national estimate.
- **Base Building:** Every family that lost coverage over a missed form, not a change in income, just learned exactly how fragile 'eligible' really is. That is an organizing conversation, not a caseload stat.
- **Educator:** Nobody in this room disputes the numbers. What they don't have is a sentence that connects a missed recertification form to a tax bill. That sentence is the entire job.
