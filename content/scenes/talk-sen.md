# talk-sen

## A · start
**Sen. X:** "I'll be honest with you — I have not read it." Sen. X says this without embarrassment, because it is week one and reading it now would be a waste of a good afternoon. "Send it when the House has finished doing whatever the House is going to do to it. I'll read the version that actually reaches me."

- **Procedure:** Cold, but correct. The Senate reads the bill that crosses over, not the one that was filed — those are frequently different documents.
- **Talk Story:** 'I have not read it' is not a brush-off, it's a schedule. Ask what would make them read it early.

### 1. "Then read the fiscal note. Four pages, and it won't change."
"Four pages I can do." They take it. Somewhere in the building that is a substantial commitment.

- effect: +4 senate
- then: end

### 2. "Fair. I'll see you in April."
"You will." It is not warmth, exactly, but it is not nothing — most advocates argue with that sentence and lose the afternoon.

- effect: +1 senate
- then: end
