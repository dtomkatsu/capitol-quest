# w13-crossover2

## A · start
Second crossover, and it is the quiet one. No gallery, no cameras, no matching shirts — everybody spent those in March. The Senate reads the House measures on third reading, the House reads the Senate's, and by Thursday the year's legislation is a fixed list that nothing can be added to.

What survives goes to conference. What does not is dead for the year, and 'for the year' is doing a lot of work in that sentence.

- **Procedure:** Carryover: this is the first year of a two-year biennium, so a measure that dies now stays alive on the file and can be taken up again next January. It does not have to be reintroduced.
- **Base Building:** Be careful how you sell carryover to people who took a day off work to testify in February. 'We'll get it next year' is true and it is also the most effective sedative in this building.
- **Policy Brain:** Next January the revenue picture will be the storm plus a year of recovery. A bill that carries over does not carry over into the same budget it was written for.

### 1. Tell the coalition the truth about carryover, all of it.
You say it plainly on the Thursday call: what carries over, what that is actually worth, and why next year is a different budget. Nobody thanks you for it. Two organizers who were about to quit do not quit.

- effect: +4 support, -1 influence
- then: B

### 2. Say nothing yet. Everything alive needs you in conference, not on a call.
The call gets postponed to next week and then to the week after. You are not wrong about where the work is. The people who filled a hearing room in February find out from a newsletter.

- effect: +3 influence, -2 support
- then: B

## B · list
Thursday evening the list closes. Whatever is on it is the year.

Conference convenes next week, behind doors, with no public testimony and no more amendments from the floor. From here on, everything that happens to your bills happens in a room you cannot enter, decided by people whose names get posted on Monday.

- **Procedure:** No testimony in conference. No public hearing on the final draft. The most consequential week of the session is the least visible one, and that is not an accident.
