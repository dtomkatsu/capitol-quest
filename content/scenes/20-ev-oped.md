# ev-oped

## A · start
Morning paper, page A1 turn: an op-ed titled 'The Last Tax Hike Hawaiʻi Can Afford,' signed by three retired executives. It names your bills. It is, annoyingly, well written.

- **Educator:** A well-written op-ed still gets the causation backwards. That's a teachable moment, if you can resist just yelling 'wrong.'
- **Talk Story:** Three retired executives against school meals is a sentence that reads itself out loud. Consider letting it.

### 1. Write the response yourself. Name the stakes, name the kids.
Your reply runs Sunday. The coalition shares it 400 times. One executive writes again; nobody reads the sequel.

- effect: +4 support, -2 influence
- then: end

### 2. Let it breathe. Op-eds don't vote.
By Thursday it's fish wrap. The building respects a campaign that doesn't chase every ball.

- effect: +2 influence
- then: end

### 3. [Talk Story 12 white] Skip the reply. Take it to live radio, unedited.
- mods: support/30
Pass:
Forty minutes, no filter, one host who lets you finish a sentence. By noon two of the three executives are being asked about THEIR taxes.

- effect: +6 support, -2 influence
Fail:
You are asked, four times, whether this is a tax increase. Four times you explain that it is complicated. The clip that travels is the fourth time.

- effect: -2 support, -3 influence
